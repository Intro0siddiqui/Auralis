# v2.6.69

**The app is usable. Downloads work again, and pause/resume works.**

Two long-running bugs closed, both confirmed on the owner's device. Neither was
solved by the theory that was written down first — in both cases the mechanism
research was good and the *premise* underneath it was never checked.

---

## 1. Pause → resume → immediate re-pause — CLOSED

**Cause:** the foreground media service never abandoned its `AudioFocusRequest`
on pause. `abandonAudioFocus()` was called only from `onDestroy()`, so every
pause left the previous request registered, and every resume layered a new one
on top of it. Android invalidated the stale entry and delivered `AUDIOFOCUS_LOSS`
on the *same listener lambda that had just been re-registered* — whose loss
branch read that as an external app stealing focus and called
`NativeBridge.command("pause")`, roughly 25 ms after every grant. Each resume
recreated the collision.

**Measured** (owner, 2026-10-03): adding `abandonAudioFocus()` to the pause branch
takes every `pause-dispatch: audio-focus-loss` dispatch to zero. Pause and resume
both work. 19 cycles per minute of re-pause → none.

**Inferred, and labelled as such:** the stale-request invalidation mechanism. The
measurement is the *absence* of the storm, not a direct observation of the
framework invalidating anything.

This also kills two earlier dead ends. `AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN → command("play")` never
fired in any device capture — the whole implicit-loop theory was inference from a
theory. And `removeFocusStackEntryOnDeath` / `event: died` was the stale entry
being reclaimed, not a garbage-collected `FocusRequester`. **No external app was
ever observed stealing focus.** Every version of this bug that blamed the device,
another app, or audio routing was wrong.

Independent fix kept alongside it: `requestAudioFocus`'s return value was being
discarded, so `DELAYED` and `FAILED` were indistinguishable from `GRANTED`. Real
defect, different failure mode, also kept.

## 2. Downloads — working again, and *not* solved

**5 files downloaded on the owner's device** where the count was 1 before, all
valid MP4:

| track | size | itag | class |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ethereal | 3.1 MB | 18 | muxed |
| фрози — Parano | 7.0 MB | 18 | muxed |
| deprezz — I'll Do It | 5.6 MB | 18 | muxed |
| GO gyal | 4.7 MB | 18 | muxed |
| otilia — bilionera | 6.9 MB | 18 | muxed |

Three fixes shipped together, and **five samples cannot separate them:**

- **The `ip=` pin is gone.** v2.6.68 read `ip=` from the URL and called
  `resolve_to_addrs(host, ip)`, which asks reqwest to reach the CDN *by dialling
  the client's own address*. Measured: connect failure on 3/3 tracks. Removing it
  cannot make things worse.
- **The retry ladder can now see transport failures.** `reqwest` reports every
  connect/DNS/TLS failure as `error sending request`, which matched no branch of
  the gate, so it did `map.delete` and returned — **zero** retries with the budget
  never consulted. v2.6.68 genuinely stopped after one attempt.
- **`CLASS_ORDER` is now `['muxed','adaptive','opus']`.** Adaptive and opus have
  now failed **5/5** on this network; every winner is muxed itag 18 from `ANDROID`.

### The 403 is not solved. Read this part.

The 403 is still there and still uniform: `403`, `ct=text/plain`, empty body,
byte 0. It now happens **less**, because the ladder reaches muxed instead of
spending its one attempt on a class that cannot work. That is routing around a
refusal, not fixing it.

**The most likely reading, still a hypothesis:** muxed-from-`ANDROID` was always
servable, and for 39 days the ladder never reached it. If so, the fix is "stop
wasting the attempt on adaptive", not "the 403 is gone".

**Measured and falsified along the way:**

- The CDN does **not** check the client's source address. Same URL, egressing from
  a *different* IPv6 address on the same device → **206**. The 403 that started
  the whole `ip=` theory was **signature validation** — `ip` is in `sparams`, so
  editing it invalidates `sig`/`lsig`.
- The PO token is **not** the cause. Audio formats are available without one and
  every one is refused at byte 0 (`ANDROID_VR audioWithUrl=4 opusWithUrl=2`,
  `IOS audioWithUrl=2`, `MWEB audioWithUrl=6` on one run).
- Excluded by measurement: source address, format class, client, headers, TLS and
  HTTP/2 fingerprint, HTTP version, `Range`, token, format availability.

The one untested difference left is the **cookie jar** — resolution runs in the
WebView with YouTube's cookies and `visitorData`, and `reqwest` sends none of it.
`examples/fetch_probe.rs` can settle the next split once a full failing URL can be
pasted into it, which is why the 160-char truncation in error messages is gone.
